The words that
hold it together.

jer · ali · da · kad... · 20 questions per round

Conjunctions are the glue between sentences you already know how to say. This page also locks down which case each common preposition wants — od, do, iz, prema, kod, u, na — one of the trickiest habits to build.

ⰀWord by word

Each one with a short example, so you can hear how it actually sits in a sentence.

And, or, neither

  • i and — the plainest connector.Imam brata i sestru.I have a brother and a sister.
  • ili or — a choice between options.Želiš čaj ili kavu?Do you want tea or coffee?
  • ni... ni neither...nor — repeat before each thing.Nemam ni vremena ni novca.I have neither time nor money.
  • a and/but — a soft contrast, often "and you?"Ja idem kući, a ti?I'm going home, and you?

Because, so

  • jer because — the reason.Ostajem doma jer pada kiša.I'm staying home because it's raining.
  • zato što because — same as jer, a touch more formal.Kasnim zato što ima gužve.I'm late because of the traffic.
  • zato so, that's why — the result, not the reason.Bilo je kasno, zato sam otišao.It was late, so I left.
  • pa so, then — the everyday word for a natural next step.Umoran sam, pa idem spavati.I'm tired, so I'm going to sleep.
  • dakle so, therefore — summing up or drawing a conclusion.Dakle, idemo?So, are we going?
Reason vs. resultjer gives the reason ("because"); zato gives the result ("so, that's why"). If you could swap in English "because", reach for jer.

But, although

  • ali but — the everyday word for contrast.Umoran sam, ali izlazim.I'm tired but I'm going out.
  • međutim however — a bit more formal, often mid-sentence.Prognoza je, međutim, bila pogrešna.The forecast, however, was wrong.
  • iako although — introduces the surprising fact.Iako pada kiša, izlazimo.Although it's raining, we're going out.
  • nego but rather — corrects or replaces what was just said.Ne želim čaj nego kavu.I don't want tea but coffee.

That

  • da that — required after verbs of thinking, saying, wanting. English can drop "that"; Croatian can't drop da.Mislim da je to dobra ideja.I think that it's a good idea.
  • da + present — "in order to", replacing the infinitive after verbs of purpose.Radim da zaradim novac.I work in order to earn money.

When, if, while

  • kad(a) when — a point in time.Kad dođem kući, nazvat ću te.When I get home, I'll call you.
  • ako if — a condition.Ako pada kiša, ostat ćemo doma.If it rains, we'll stay home.
  • dok while — two things at the same time.Dok kuham, ti se odmori.While I'm cooking, you can rest.
  • dok ne until — the extra ne stays even though it isn't negative in meaning.Čekaj dok se ne vratim.Wait until I come back.

Except, instead

  • osim except, besides — takes the genitive.Svi su došli osim Ane.Everyone came except Ana.
  • umjesto instead of — also takes the genitive.Uzet ću čaj umjesto kave.I'll have tea instead of coffee.

ⰁPrepositions & their case

The part that trips people up isn't the meaning — it's remembering which case has to follow.

  • od+ genitive — from (a person/origin), or "than" in comparisons.Ovaj poklon je od moje mame.This gift is from my mother.
  • do+ genitive — until, up to.Trgovina je otvorena do osam.The shop is open until eight.
  • iz+ genitive — out of, from inside a place.Dolazim iz škole.I'm coming from school.
  • s / sa+ genitive (off a surface) — or + instrumental (with).Dolazim s posla.I'm coming from work.
  • prema+ dative — toward, in the direction of.Idem prema kolodvoru.I'm walking toward the station.
  • k / ka+ dative — toward, mostly for people. In speech prema is far more common.Dolazim k tebi.I'm coming to you.
  • kod+ genitive — at/to someone's place.Idem kod bake.I'm going to Grandma's place.
  • u+ accusative (motion in) or + locative (location inside).Idem u grad. Ja sam u gradu.I'm going into town. I'm in town.
  • na+ accusative (motion onto) or + locative (location on/at).Idem na posao. Ja sam na poslu.I'm going to work. I'm at work.
The departure pairWhichever preposition takes you to a place decides which one brings you back. u (into) pairs with iz (out of): u kuhinju ↔ iz kuhinje. na (onto) pairs with s/sa (off): na posao ↔ s posla. Know how you got there, and you know how to say you left.

Full noun endings for each case live on their own pages — Genitive and Dative cover the two that show up most in this list.
